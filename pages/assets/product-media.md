# Product demonstration media

The 50-second film follows a research team from a planning conversation through a published paper to a draft of its next experiment. The meeting and the illustrated Codex conversation are fictional demonstration content. The research results are from the published paper.

## Research source

Keigo Minamida, Koya Dendo, Yuto Nakamura, and Jun Rekimoto. *Warping the Workspace: Expanding Visual Access with Adjustable Reach Mapping for Humanoid Teleoperation*. UIST Adjunct, 2026. The film uses arXiv v1, including Figure 1, Table 1 and the results sentence from the abstract. Global Zoom had 23 completed trials out of 24. Six of eight participants preferred Focus+Context. [Paper and license notice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28712) · [arXiv DOI](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.28712) · [ACM DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3830397.3841893) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The graphics crop and reframe the figures, table and text. The film highlights two cells from the original table. The Japanese in the parallel reader was generated by Apple Translation inside kAIgi.

## Native screens and edits

The recording, Library and Ask views come from a signed macOS development build of kAIgi 0.7.5. The app ran with an isolated home, its own recording and document libraries, and network access disabled. The public arXiv PDF was downloaded separately and imported into that library. The address close-up shows the DOI entered into the real Library field. The edit joins that entry to the imported PDF.

| Start | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00 | The two voices decide which measures to record. The Mic and Speaker excerpts come from the real two-column view of the synthetic recording. |
| 0:09 | The Library displays the accessible paper, the original sentence and its Japanese translation. Table 1 compares completion counts with preference votes. |
| 0:28 | Ask finds the meeting. Opening the first candidate leads to the recording, where the decision is visible at 00:09. |
| 0:40 | In an illustrated Codex conversation, a researcher asks in Japanese for an experiment plan based on the meeting and paper. The example draft cites the recording at 00:09–00:15 and Table 1. |

The synthetic meeting is named 次の遠隔操作実験. For the question “次の実験、何を記録するんだっけ？”, Ask returned the microphone question at 00:04 as its first recording candidate. The recording also contains the speaker’s reply at 00:09. These are captured native results. The film crops the relevant controls and animates the pointer, typing, and transitions. The voices and the transcript are staged for the example.

A separate debug CLI process searched the same isolated recording using `kaigi context "記録" --limit 2`. It returned `R1-E2` with channel `speaker`, `start_seconds` 9, `end_seconds` 15 and the text “課題を終えた回数と、どの表示が好みか。別々に記録しよう。” This local result and the published table supply the references shown in the final shot. The prompt and response were scripted and the Codex conversation was typeset for the film.

- `product-capture.webp` is the actual recording window. `product-capture-detail.webp` crops its Speaker column from the same session.
- `recording-conversation.webp` uses the film’s two-input composition at 00:07.3. The narrow-screen view uses its toolbar crop, `recording-controls.webp`, and renders the two original utterances as selectable text.
- `product-library.webp` is the captured paper and companion translation view. `product-translation-detail.webp` crops its native inspector.
- `product-ask.webp` crops a frame at 00:32.6 of the film. It shows the Ask query and its first recording candidate (Mic, 00:04).
- `promo-poster.webp` and `ogp-product.png` are editorial compositions. They arrange the short paper title, author names, Figure 1, the native Library address field and the translated excerpt for the site's cover.
- The older `product-notes.webp` and `product-notes-detail.webp` illustrate a saved note from a separate fictional reading document in kAIgi 0.5.5. They are separate from this film.

## Sound and captions

The eight Japanese speech clips were generated with 音読さん. Only the scripted lines were submitted. The final scene displays the voice credit throughout. The film has a 48 kHz mono AAC track, H.264 video at 1440 × 810 and 24 frames per second, and matched Japanese and English WebVTT tracks. Both caption tracks include a final, unvoiced summary of the on-screen draft. The MP4 metadata also credits the paper, its license and the speech provider.
